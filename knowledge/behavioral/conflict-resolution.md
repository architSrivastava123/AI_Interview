# Behavioral Interviews: Technical Disagreements and Conflict Resolution

Interviewers ask about technical disagreements to assess maturity, empathy, collaborative decision-making, and constructive compromise.

## Core Principles for Answering Conflict Questions
1. **Focus on the Problem, Not the Person**: Frame disagreements around technical trade-offs, scalability constraints, or user experience rather than interpersonal tension.
2. **Seek Understanding First**: Actively listen to the other engineer's perspective, identify their core concerns, and validate their technical rationale.
3. **Use Data and Prototypes**: When architectural opinions diverge, propose benchmarks, Proof-of-Concepts (POCs), or metrics-driven tests to evaluate options objectively.
4. **Disagree and Commit**: Once a final team consensus or lead decision is made, fully support execution without lingering resentment.

## Recommended Response Structure
- Describe the architectural debate (e.g., GraphQL vs REST, SQL vs NoSQL, monorepo vs multirepo).
- Explain how you presented data, listened to alternative concerns, and facilitated a compromise or spike test.
- State the successful project delivery outcome and long-term positive relationship with the teammate.
